Instagram DMs for sales: how to organize your inbox and stop losing orders

Right now there's probably an order sitting in an Instagram folder nobody on your team opens. Here's where sales disappear in DMs and a routine that stops them slipping away.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·October 19, 2026·6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Messages from people who don't follow you land in Requests, and plenty of sales die there unseen.
  • Answering the price in the DM, with a next step attached, sells more than hiding it.
  • Tools connected to Meta's API work within a reply window, 24 hours in most cases.
  • Moving the chat to WhatsApp is only worth it when it gains something concrete; otherwise it's one more step to drop off.

Saturday, 4 p.m. A clothing boutique posts a story with a new dress. Within two hours, 23 DMs arrive: "price?", "do you have M?", a fire emoji, "do you ship to the suburbs?", "want it!". The owner answers the ones she sees on her phone between customers. On Monday she finds nine more in the requests folder. One of them wanted three pieces.

That's what using Instagram DMs for sales looks like: buying intent shows up fast and cools down fast. Whoever replies right away sells. Whoever keeps the DMs organized doesn't lose the rest.

This guide covers the operational side: where orders come from, where they hide, how to answer the classic "price?" and when it makes sense to move the conversation to WhatsApp.

Where do orders come from on Instagram?

Orders rarely arrive as "hello, I'd like to buy". They arrive like this:

  • Story replies. The customer reacts or types over a story. The message lands in DMs with a story thumbnail that disappears after 24 hours along with the story.
  • Post comments. "Price?", "in blue?", tagging a friend. Many people ask publicly and expect a public answer.
  • Direct DMs. Sometimes with a screenshot of another post, sometimes just "hi".
  • Ads. The customer taps an ad that opens a DM and arrives with a ready-made question.

Each source needs different care. A story reply has to name the product, because tomorrow nobody will remember. A comment has to become a private conversation. A bare "hi" needs a question back.

Why do so many sales get lost in DMs?

In nearly every business I know that sells on Instagram, the leaks are the same:

  • The Requests folder. Messages from people who don't follow you go to a separate folder and aren't even marked as seen until someone accepts them. New customers who just discovered your brand are exactly the ones who don't follow you yet.
  • One login, several people. The social media manager, the owner and the salesperson open the same profile on different phones. One reads, another assumes it was answered, nobody replies.
  • Read means forgotten. DMs have no "pending" state. Opened, grayed out, gone from memory.
  • Chats sent to WhatsApp that never arrive. "Text me on WhatsApp" and the customer never does.

None of these is about effort. They're about process. And process gets fixed with simple rules.

How to answer "price?" without losing the sale

Here's my opinion: hiding prices in DMs is a bad habit. A customer asking "price?" is one step from buying or leaving. Replying "message us on WhatsApp" or "sent you a DM" (on a public comment, without sending anything) adds friction at the worst moment.

A good reply has three parts: the price, a detail that helps them decide and a question that pulls the next step.

Hi Carla! The Luna dress is US$49, and we can split it into 3 payments. We have S, M and L in black and green. What size do you wear? I'll check if we can ship today.

For a story reply, start by naming the product:

Hi! About the caramel bag in today's story: genuine leather, 12 x 9 in, US$69. Want me to send a few photos of the inside?

And for a loose "hi", a question that makes it easy for them:

Hi there, glad you stopped by. Did you see a specific piece, or would you like help choosing something?

Save these as saved replies in Instagram itself or in your team's tool. Tweak the name and product on the spot, and the reply goes out in seconds, consistent no matter who sends it.

Comments and story replies: turning them into conversations

A comment asking about price is a great signal. But sales talk doesn't work in comments: nobody posts their address publicly, and other followers watch the negotiation.

Reply briefly in the comment and move to private right away:

Hi Jen! Just sent you the details in DMs.

Then actually send the DM. It sounds obvious, but "sent you a DM" with nothing in the DMs is a classic. Make one person responsible for sweeping comments on the last three days of posts, twice a day. Older posts get questions too.

Instagram's 24-hour rule for businesses

In the Instagram app, you can reply to a DM whenever you like. When a business uses a tool connected to Meta's API, though, the rules change: there's a standard messaging window that starts when the customer writes. According to Meta's official Instagram messaging documentation, for most user actions this window lasts 24 hours. When the first message comes from an ad that opens a DM, it can last up to 7 days.

There's also a tag that lets a human agent reply after the window in specific situations, under Meta's own rules. Don't count on it for everyday sales.

In practice, the rule mirrors WhatsApp: reply fast and end each message with a question, so the customer answers and the conversation stays open. If you already use the WhatsApp API, the logic is close to the WhatsApp 24-hour window.

The same documentation includes a useful detail: requests answered through a third-party tool move out of Requests into the General folder. Centralizing your inbox also pulls orders out of that forgotten folder.

Move the chat to WhatsApp or stay in DMs?

Many businesses push everyone from Instagram to WhatsApp out of habit. Sometimes it makes sense; sometimes it just adds a step where customers drop off.

SituationWhere to continue
Quick price or size questionDM
Undecided, still browsingDM
Payment, receipt, invoiceWhatsApp
Delivery, tracking, after-salesWhatsApp
Long quote with filesWhatsApp
Customer asked for WhatsAppWhatsApp

The logic: DMs are great at the start, while the customer is still in the mood of the post. WhatsApp is better for what comes after, because that's where customers follow their order and where you can reach them later with an approved template.

When you switch channels, build the bridge yourself. Instead of "text me on WhatsApp", ask for their number and write first, or send a WhatsApp link with a pre-filled message. And if switching gains nothing clear, don't switch.

Start with the folder nobody opens

Today, before anything else, open your business's Requests folder. Count how many messages are there and how old the oldest unanswered one is.

If the number bothers you, you know where to start. Answer the last few days' worth, with the price and a question, and make someone the owner of that folder starting tomorrow.

Frequently asked questions

Where do messages from non-followers go on Instagram?

Into the Requests folder of your DMs. They don't show up in the main inbox and aren't marked as seen until you accept them, which is why orders often sit there for days.

Is there a deadline to reply to an Instagram DM?

Not in the app itself. But when a business replies through a tool connected to Meta's API, a standard messaging window applies, which in most cases is 24 hours after the customer's last message.

Is it better to sell through Instagram DMs or WhatsApp?

It depends on the customer and the stage. DMs are great for first contact from a post or story; WhatsApp tends to work better for payment, delivery and after-sales. Avoid forcing customers to switch apps without a reason.

Should I post prices in DMs or ask customers to message me on WhatsApp?

Answer the price in the DM. Asking someone to switch apps just to learn the price adds friction, and some people simply give up.
