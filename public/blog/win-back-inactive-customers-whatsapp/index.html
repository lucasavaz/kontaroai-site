How to win back inactive customers on WhatsApp with one message (templates by industry)

Your past customers already know where you are and what you charge. Often all they need is a reason. The hard part is giving one without sounding desperate.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·October 23, 2026·8 min read

Key takeaways

  • An inactive customer is someone past your normal buying cycle, not someone gone for a fixed number of days.
  • Split inactive customers into groups and write one message per group. One offer to the whole list means blocks and wasted money.
  • Only message people who agreed to hear from you. Win-back is marketing and needs an approved template.
  • Do the math first: in a hypothetical example, two returning customers pay for a send to 500 people.
  • Measure returns in days, not just replies, and compare against a group that got no message.

Every business has a list nobody looks at: people who bought, liked it, and simply stopped showing up. Knowing how to win back inactive customers on WhatsApp often pays better than chasing new ones, because these people already trust you. They don't need convincing that you exist, only that coming back is worth it.

The classic mistake is opening the whole list and sending "We miss you! 20% OFF" to everyone. It works poorly, gets you blocked and costs real money. You can do much better with a single message, as long as it goes to the right person.

What counts as an inactive customer in your business?

There's no magic number of days. A pizzeria and a mattress store run on completely different rhythms. What defines inactivity is your buying cycle: the normal gap between one purchase and the next.

A practical rule I use: inactive means past twice the normal gap. If a salon client comes back every 30 days, 60 days without a visit is a warning sign.

The figures below are hypothetical starting points for you to adjust with your own data:

BusinessNormal gap (example)Inactive after
Restaurant with delivery2 weeks1 month
Salon, barbershop, beauty1 month2 months
Clothing store3 months6 months
Gym, school, courseActive membershipPlan ended 1 month ago

To find your own gap, take 20 regular customers and look at the time between each one's last two purchases. Put them in order and take the middle value. That's a solid number to start with.

How to split inactive customers into groups

Someone who drifted off six weeks ago and someone gone for two years shouldn't get the same message. I use three groups:

  • Cooling off (1 to 2 times the cycle): still remembers you. A reminder or something new is usually enough.
  • Inactive (2 to 4 times the cycle): needs a concrete reason. A perk can make sense here.
  • Lost (more than 4 times the cycle): reply odds drop and block odds rise. Think hard before sending.

Within each group, cross it with what the person bought and how much they used to spend. A client who got highlights every two months deserves a different message from someone who came in once for a blowout.

And remove, no exceptions: anyone who asked to opt out, anyone who complained in their last conversation, and anyone who never agreed to receive messages.

Can I message old customers on WhatsApp?

Yes, if they agreed to it. On the official platform, a win-back message is marketing: its goal is to make the person buy again. That brings two requirements.

The first is permission. Under the WhatsApp Business opt-in rules, a business must make clear that the person is opting in to receive messages and name the business they'll hear from, and it must comply with applicable law. That includes data protection laws such as Brazil's LGPD or Europe's GDPR. Keep a record of when and how each customer agreed.

The second is an approved template. An inactive customer, by definition, hasn't talked to you in more than 24 hours, so the conversation can only start with a Meta-approved marketing template. Don't try to pass a win-back off as utility: Meta reclassifies it, and the guide on rejected WhatsApp templates explains why.

If you haven't built your opt-in list yet, start with the guide to WhatsApp marketing messages without getting blocked.

Win-back message templates by industry

Every template follows the same structure: who's writing, a reason tied to the person, an easy action, and a clear way out. Replace whatever is in brackets. The business names are made up.

Restaurants and delivery

Hi [name]! It's Stone Oven Pizza. Your [favorite pizza] hasn't left our oven in a while.

Until Sunday, the stuffed crust is on us with your next order. Want me to put one down for you?

If you'd rather not get our messages, reply STOP.

It works because it mentions their order, not a generic menu. And the perk has a real, short deadline.

Salons, barbershops and beauty

Hi [name], how are you? It's Carla from Studio Bella. Your last [service] was in [month], and this is usually when it's time for a touch-up.

I have openings with [stylist] Thursday afternoon and Saturday morning. Want me to hold one for you?

To stop getting messages, just reply STOP.

Notice there's no discount. For the "cooling off" group, a reminder with an open slot is often enough, and you keep your margin.

Clothing and shoe stores

Hi [name]! It's Julia from Avenue Boutique. The new [category they bought] collection just arrived, and I set aside three pieces in your size ([size]) that go with what you picked up last time.

Want me to send you photos here?

If you'd rather not get updates, reply STOP.

The ask is tiny: just look at photos. Saying yes to that is much easier than to "come visit the store."

Gyms, schools and courses

Hi [name]. It's Ryan from Motion Gym. I saw your membership ended in [month].

We've opened a [class type] class at [time they used to come], which was your slot. Want to try a free class this week, no strings attached?

To stop getting messages, reply STOP.

People who leave a gym or course almost always leave because of routine, not price. Showing a time that fits their day tackles the real objection.

A version for customers gone a long time

Hi [name]. It's Lucas from [business]. It's been a while, and I'd like to ask: was there something we got wrong last time?

If you'd like to come back, your next [purchase/visit] includes [perk] until [date]. And if you'd rather not hear from us, reply STOP and I'll take you off the list.

Asking what went wrong feels risky, but it tends to get honest answers. Some turn into sales. The rest turn into lessons.

What does a win-back campaign cost, and how many customers need to return?

Here's a hypothetical example: a salon with 500 inactive clients who agreed to receive messages, all in Brazil.

  • On Meta's rate card in effect since October 1, 2026, a marketing message to Brazil costs US$0.0625. Other countries have different rates.
  • 500 × US$0.0625 = US$31.25.
  • If the salon's average ticket is US$25 (example), two returning clients cover the send. Counting only margin, say 50%, it takes three.

Now compare that with the urge to message the whole base, including people who haven't come in for years. For 3,000 contacts, the bill climbs to US$187.50. And the oldest contacts are exactly the ones who reply least and block most.

Meta's rates change by quarter and by country. Before you send, check the official pricing page and simulate it in the Meta cost calculator. I did a fuller breakdown, including platform and AI, in the guide on how much the WhatsApp Business API costs.

How to tell whether the win-back worked

A reply isn't a sale. A campaign can get lots of "aww, miss you guys!" and nobody coming back. Track four numbers:

MetricHow to calculate it
Reply ratePeople who replied ÷ people who received it
Return ratePeople who bought within 30 days ÷ people who received it
Opt-out ratePeople who replied STOP ÷ people who received it
Revenue per dollar spentRevenue from returners ÷ cost of the send

One habit makes a real difference: set aside about 10% of the list and send them nothing. After 30 days, compare. If 4% of the group that got the message came back and 3% of the group without it also came back, the campaign's real effect was 1 point, not 4. Without that comparison group, you give the message credit for customers who would have returned anyway.

If opt-outs spike, the problem is the group or the message. Fix it before the next one.

Before you send a win-back campaign

Start with 30 customers, not 3,000

Here's what I'd do this week: pick the 30 customers who bought the most and drifted off recently. Write one message for them, get the template approved, and send it on a Tuesday or Wednesday, when the whole team is around to reply.

Within a week, you'll know whether the wording works, which objection comes up, and what each returning customer is worth. Then it makes sense to scale. And if you're unsure who to message first, read your latest conversations: very often the customer disappeared right after a question nobody answered.

Frequently asked questions

When is a customer considered inactive?

It depends on your buying cycle. A practical rule is to treat as inactive anyone past twice their normal interval between purchases. At a salon where a client comes back every 30 days, 60 days without a visit is a warning sign.

Can I message a customer on WhatsApp who bought years ago?

Only if they agreed to receive messages from your business and haven't opted out. Even with permission, very old contacts tend to block more. Start with the most recent ones.

Should I offer a discount to bring customers back?

Not always. For people who drifted away recently, a useful reminder or something new is often enough. Save discounts for those who've been gone longer, and test with a small group first.

Is a win-back message marketing or utility?

Marketing. Inviting someone to buy again is promotional, so it needs a template approved in the marketing category and the customer's permission.
