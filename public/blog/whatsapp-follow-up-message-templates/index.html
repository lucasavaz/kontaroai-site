WhatsApp follow-up messages: ready-to-copy templates that don't sound pushy

The customer went quiet and you don't want to sound desperate. The difference between a pushy follow-up and one that sells comes down to one thing, and it shows up in every template below.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·November 9, 2026·7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good follow-up brings something new: information, an option, a real deadline. “Any thoughts?” brings nothing.
  • Each situation needs a different message: unanswered quote, “I'll think about it”, silence after the price, held items and after-sales.
  • Making it easy to say no gets more replies, including more yeses.
  • After 24 hours without a customer message, the official API only allows approved templates, and purchase reminders usually count as marketing.
  • Two or three attempts are enough. After that, close the loop gracefully.

You sent the quote on Tuesday. Good photos, clear terms, a friendly “any questions?”. Silence. Now it's Thursday and your thumb is hovering over the keyboard, trying to figure out how to write without it sounding like you're chasing a debt.

This is a library of WhatsApp follow-up message templates, organized by where the customer stopped. The routine of when to follow up (the board, the rhythm, the daily review) is in our guide on how to turn WhatsApp conversations into sales. This one is about the words.

Why so many follow-ups sound pushy

A pushy follow-up has one trait: it only reminds the customer that they owe you a reply. “Hi, did you get a chance to look?”, “Any thoughts?”, “Any news?”. None of these gives the customer a new reason to answer. They just add guilt, and guilt makes people avoid the conversation.

The templates below follow three simple rules:

  1. Bring something new. A missing photo, a cheaper second option, the answer to a question left hanging, a deadline that's real.
  2. Be specific. Mention what the customer asked for. “The quote for the 3-seat sofa” shows you remember them. “Our quote” reads like a mass blast.
  3. Make it easy to say no. When customers feel they can decline without awkwardness, they reply. And some of those replies are yes.

Names, products and amounts in the templates are examples. Swap in your own and match the way your business talks.

Quote sent, no reply

The most common case. The customer asked, you sent it, nothing. Most of the time they haven't decided against you: they just haven't decided.

First follow-up, one business day later, offering concrete help:

Hi Patricia! About the quote for the 3-seat sofa: a lot of people go back and forth between the linen and the suede. If you'd like, I can send you photos of both side by side in daylight so you can see the color difference.

It works because it offers something that helps her decide, without asking whether she has decided.

Second follow-up, a few days later, with an alternative:

Patricia, one thought: if the price came in above what you planned, the same model in a 2-seater is US$350 less and fits the same wall. Want me to put that quote together too?

Here you address the most likely reason for the silence (price) without accusing anyone.

When the customer says “I'll think about it”

“I'll think about it” is rarely just that. Sometimes it means “I need to talk to my partner”, sometimes “it's expensive”, sometimes “I'm still shopping around”. The mistake is to accept it and disappear, or to push right then.

Reply in the same conversation, before it cools:

Of course, no rush! So I don't bother you at a bad time: when would be a good moment for me to check back? And if any question would help you decide, just send it here.

You get two things: an agreed date (the follow-up stops being an intrusion and becomes a commitment) and an open door for the real objection to come out.

On the agreed day:

Hi Robert! We said we'd talk today about the English course. The Tuesday and Thursday evening group still has 3 spots. Does that work for you, or should I show you another schedule?

The message recalls what you agreed and adds new, true information. If spots aren't actually limited, don't make it up: fake urgency burns trust the first time a customer notices.

Went silent after the price

The customer was excited, asked about color, timing, shipping. You sent the price and they vanished. It's the most telling silence, and the easiest to handle honestly.

Hi Fiona! I noticed our chat stopped after I sent the price. If it came in higher than you expected, just tell me, no problem at all: I have options at other price points and we can split it into 6 interest-free payments.

If you can't move on price, shift the focus to what's included:

Fiona, one thing that may not have been clear: the price already includes installation and a 1-year labor warranty. Many quotes charge for those separately.

And if it's the last attempt, close gracefully. This message gets a surprising number of replies, because it removes all the pressure:

Fiona, I'll stop messaging so I don't crowd your inbox. If it makes sense later on, just say hi here and I'll pick up where we left off. Thanks for the chat!

Held item or abandoned order

An item set aside, a table booked, a tentative appointment, an order put together but not paid. Here the follow-up has a natural deadline, and you can use it without drama.

Hi Luke! I've set aside the black sneakers in size 10 for you until tomorrow at 6 p.m. Should I send you the payment link, or would you rather pick them up and pay in store?

The question offers two paths to yes. Nobody has to explain anything.

For an order that's ready but unpaid:

Hi Camille! Your order with the 2 skincare kits is packed and ready. Did something go wrong with the payment? If it helps, I can send you a new payment link right now.

Assuming there might have been a technical problem is kind, and often true.

After-sales: the follow-up almost nobody does

The customer bought. Most businesses consider the job done. But after-sales is the follow-up most likely to get a reply, because the person already trusts you.

A few days after delivery:

Hi Beth! Did the table arrive in good shape? If any piece came damaged or assembly was a hassle, let me know here and we'll sort it out.

After some time of use, when it makes sense to ask for a review or referral:

Beth, so glad you like the table! If you know anyone shopping for furniture, feel free to pass along my number. And if you'd leave a review on our profile, it really helps a small shop like ours.

A problem solved in after-sales becomes a loyal customer. A problem discovered only in a public review becomes a one-star rating.

Past 24 hours: what changes on the official API

If you use the official WhatsApp API, remember one rule: after 24 hours without a message from the customer, you can only write using a Meta-approved template. We explain it all in how the WhatsApp 24-hour window works.

In practice, that means having a few templates ready before you need them, and knowing how Meta will categorize each one. According to Meta's template categorization rules, utility is for non-promotional messages tied to something the customer requested or did, like an order confirmation. Cart reminders are treated as marketing, even when the customer asked for them. And a template that mixes an update with an offer also becomes marketing.

In other words: a quote follow-up outside the window will almost always be a marketing template, which requires the customer's opt-in and costs more per delivered message. To get a sense of the cost in your case, use the Meta cost calculator.

An example of a short template that invites a reply and reopens the conversation:

Hi [name]! I still have your quote for [product] saved here. Can I answer any questions or update the prices for you?

The best way to save money here is not needing the template at all: end every message with a question, so the customer replies while the window is still open.

Pick three templates and test them this week

Don't try to use everything at once. Open last week's stalled conversations and sort them into two groups: unanswered quotes and “I'll think about it”. Adapt one template for each group, plus the closing message, and send them today to five customers in each group.

On Friday, count the replies. Not just the yeses: count the nos too. A clear no frees up your time and cleans up your board. Endless silence is the only result that teaches you nothing.

Frequently asked questions

How many follow-up messages should I send before giving up?

Two or three, spaced out, each with a new reason to reply. After that, send a polite closing message and leave the door open.

How long should I wait before the first follow-up?

For a quote, one business day usually works well. For someone who said “I'll think about it”, ask when it makes sense to check back and stick to that date.

Can I follow up on WhatsApp after 24 hours?

On the official API, yes, but only with a Meta-approved template and with the customer's opt-in. Reminders to complete a purchase are usually categorized as marketing, the most expensive category.

How do I follow up without being annoying?

Bring something useful in every message, keep it short, mention what the customer asked for and end with a question that's easy to answer, even with a no.
