A customer sent a voice note on WhatsApp: reply with text or audio?

The two-minute voice note nobody on the team can play right now costs more than it looks. The fix isn't banning audio, it's deciding what goes in each format.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·October 26, 2026·7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Reply in the format that solves the issue, not the format the customer happened to use.
  • Prices, addresses, hours, deadlines and payment details always go in writing, even if you also send audio.
  • Transcribing incoming voice notes gets the team off the headphones and makes conversations searchable.
  • A good audio reply is under 40 seconds, covers one topic and ends with a clear next step.

Monday, 9:40 a.m. The salesperson at a furniture store opens WhatsApp and finds three voice notes from the same customer: one at 1:52, another at 47 seconds and a last one of 8 seconds that just says "so, did you check?". She's at the counter with another customer in front of her. No way to listen now.

When a customer sends a voice note on WhatsApp, the question comes up right away: do I reply with text or audio? And before that, how do I listen to all this without stopping everything else?

Short answer: reply in the format that solves the issue, not the format the customer chose. The longer answer comes with a few practical rules that prevent rework, misunderstandings and customers left waiting.

Why do customers prefer sending voice notes?

Because it's easier for them. Someone driving, cooking, on the bus or who simply hates typing gets everything done in one recording. Some people struggle with writing and feel more confident speaking. Others just want to calmly explain a problem that would take five paragraphs.

None of that is wrong. Customers have no obligation to make your team's life easier.

The catch is that audio shifts the work: what was quick for the speaker becomes slow for whoever has to listen, understand, take notes and reply. Your job is to absorb that difference without the customer noticing.

What's the problem with voice notes for the team?

A solo owner answering from their phone can cope. The trouble grows when a team shares one number:

  • You can't listen anywhere. Busy front desk, a meeting, a customer at the counter. The note waits, and "later" is sometimes an hour.
  • You can't skim. You get a five-line text in ten seconds. A two-minute note takes two minutes, minimum.
  • You can't search. Next week, when someone asks "what size couch did he want?", nobody is replaying 14 voice notes.
  • You can't copy. Address, order number, tax ID: all of it gets retyped, with room for mistakes.
  • It doesn't hand over well. When a colleague takes over, they inherit a pile of recordings instead of a summary.

Quick math, as a hypothetical example: a store gets 30 voice notes a day, averaging 50 seconds. That's 25 minutes of pure listening, not counting replays to catch a missed detail. Over 22 working days, it adds up to more than 9 hours a month.

Text or audio: when should you use each?

Use this table as a house rule. It fits on a sticky note next to the screen.

SituationBest formatWhy
Price, quote, shippingTextThey'll check and compare
Address, hours, linkTextNeeds copying or tapping
Deadline, payment detailsTextAvoids "but you said..."
How to use somethingAudio + text summaryClearer when spoken
Upset customerShort audioTone of voice calms
Older customer, or one who reads poorlyAudioRespects their way
Thank-you, after-saleShort audioFeels personal

The golden rule lives in the first three rows: anything with a number or a commitment goes in writing. Send a voice note explaining calmly if you like, then confirm in text. Anyone who has argued with a customer over "I understood it was $1,200 upfront" knows why.

How do you transcribe voice notes without slowing the team?

Transcription solves most of the problem. With audio turned into text, an agent reads it in ten seconds, finds the detail with search a week later and hands the chat to a colleague without retelling the story.

There are two routes:

  1. In the WhatsApp app. The app can transcribe voice messages on the receiving phone. It works if you answer alone from your phone, but each person has to switch it on, and the transcript isn't saved for the team.
  2. In your inbox platform. When you use the official API with a shared inbox, the transcript can sit right inside the conversation, under the audio, for anyone who opens it.

A tip: read the transcript, but listen when it matters. Transcription gets proper names, fast-spoken numbers and words over background noise wrong. If the customer dictated an address or an amount, confirm in writing before acting.

Just to confirm what I got from your voice note: delivery to 214 Acacia Street, apt 32, Saturday morning. Is that right?

That confirmation takes twenty seconds and saves a truck coming back with the couch.

How do you record an audio reply that doesn't annoy?

When audio is the right call, do it well. Bad business voice notes share the same flaws: they open with "hi, how are you, all good, so...", run past a minute and end without saying what happens next.

A script that works:

  1. Name and context in one sentence. "Hi Sarah, it's Julia from the store, about the table you asked about."
  2. One topic only. Two topics means two short notes, or text with bullets.
  3. Under 40 seconds. If it runs longer, the content probably belongs in writing.
  4. Next step at the end. "I'm sending the measurements and price in writing now. Confirm by tomorrow and it goes on Friday's delivery."

And record somewhere quiet. It sounds obvious, but a blender or traffic in the background signals carelessness, however friendly the voice.

After the audio, send the summary:

As I mentioned in the audio:

Lina table, 63 in, natural wood top
US$480 by bank transfer, or 10 card installments of US$50
Friday delivery if you confirm by 6 p.m. tomorrow

What happens when an AI agent gets the voice note?

This is where many businesses hesitate. "If I turn on an AI agent, what happens when a customer sends audio?" It depends on the tool. Some ignore the audio or reply "I can't listen to audio, could you type?". That's the worst outcome: the customer who chose to speak is exactly the one least willing to type.

The right approach is for the AI to transcribe the note, understand the request and answer with what it knows about your business. In Kontaro, for instance, the AI transcribes incoming voice notes and can reply with a voice note when the customer sent audio, with the transcript visible to the team in the same conversation.

Two rules still apply with AI:

  • Numbers and commitments in writing. Even if the AI answers by voice, price, deadline and address should also appear as text.
  • Long, emotional recordings go to a person. A customer who recorded three minutes of complaints doesn't want a machine reply. Set a handoff for these cases. We cover how to decide in when AI answers alone and when it calls a person.

A technical detail that helps: through the official API, a message sent as a voice note shows up with the microphone icon and, depending on the customer's phone settings, even with a transcript underneath, as Meta's documentation on audio messages describes. So your audio can be read, too.

If your AI's tone already sounds stiff in text, it will sound worse in voice. Fix that first with the tips in how to use AI on WhatsApp without sounding like a robot.

Start with today's voice notes

Pull your last ten conversations that included audio. For each, write down how long the customer waited, whether the reply was text or audio, and whether there was a misunderstanding afterward.

In my experience, the pattern shows up fast. The problems almost always sit in conversations where a price or a deadline was agreed only by voice. Fix that first with the "numbers go in writing" rule, and the rest of the policy follows naturally.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to answer a voice note with text?

No. Most customers want the right answer, fast, in a form they can check later. If the topic involves numbers, an address or a deadline, text is often the kinder choice.

How do I transcribe WhatsApp voice notes for a business team?

The WhatsApp app can transcribe voice notes for the person receiving them, but each agent has to turn it on in their own phone. Platforms built on the official API can show the transcript inside the conversation for the whole team.

Can an AI agent reply to a customer who sent audio?

Yes, if the tool transcribes the audio. The AI reads the transcript, answers with what it learned about your business and can even reply with a short voice note, while keeping key details in writing.

How long should a customer service voice note be?

Around 40 seconds or less, one topic per note. If you need longer than that, the content probably works better as text with bullet points.
