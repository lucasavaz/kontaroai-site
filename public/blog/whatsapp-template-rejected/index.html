WhatsApp template rejected? Why Meta says no and how to write one that gets approved

Most of the time the problem isn't what you want to say. It's where a curly brace, a variable or a coupon ended up. You can usually fix it in ten minutes.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·October 30, 2026·7 min read

Key takeaways

  • There are two kinds of no: a rejected template, and a template approved in a different category, usually marketing.
  • Badly formatted variables, variables at the start or end of the text, and too many variables are among the reasons Meta itself lists.
  • Any promotional content inside a utility update turns the template into marketing.
  • You can edit and resubmit, or appeal. Meta says it decides appeals within 24 hours.
  • Category changes made after approval can be appealed within 60 days in Business Support Home.

Monday, 8:10 a.m. The receptionist at a dental office opens the dashboard to send this week's reminders and sees it: WhatsApp template rejected. Or worse, approved, but as marketing, costing several times more per message. The reminders that should go out this morning will have to wait.

I've been there more often than I'd like. The good news: the problem is almost always small and has a name. Meta publishes its rejection reasons, and they repeat.

Why was my WhatsApp template rejected?

First, tell the two kinds of "no" apart, because the fix is different:

  • Rejected. The template can't be used. Usually because of formatting, content that breaks WhatsApp's policies, or a duplicate of another template you already have.
  • Approved in a different category. You asked for utility and Meta approved it as marketing. It works, but each send costs more and follows the rules for promotional messages.

The review combines automated systems and manual checks, and Meta's template review documentation says a decision can take up to 24 hours. That's why the worst moment to discover a template won't pass is the moment you need it.

Which category should I pick: marketing, utility or authentication?

Half the headaches come from choosing the wrong category. Meta's rule is simpler than it looks:

CategoryWhen to use itExample
UtilitySomething the customer asked for or did, nothing promotionalOrder confirmed, appointment booked, invoice
MarketingOffers, news, invitations to come back, or any mixSale, launch, "we miss you"
AuthenticationOne-time codesLogin code

On its template categorization page, Meta says utility templates are typically triggered by a user action or request and can't have promotional or persuasive intent. It's also clear about mixing: an order update with a promotion attached is marketing. Vague templates, like one that's only a variable or just "Congratulations!", land in marketing too.

When in doubt, ask yourself: if I removed this template, would the customer miss information about something they did? If yes, it's utility. If the message exists to make them buy, it's marketing, and that's fine.

The mistakes that get templates rejected most

Meta's official list has four groups. In plain terms:

Broken variables

  • Missing or mismatched braces, like {{1} or {1}}.
  • Special characters inside a variable, such as #, $ or %.
  • Variables out of order: {{1}}, {{2}}, {{5}}, {{4}}.
  • Too many variables for the amount of text. A template that's mostly blanks doesn't show what will actually be sent.
  • Text that starts or ends with a variable. Meta calls these dangling parameters and won't accept them.

Content and policy

Products banned by WhatsApp's Commerce Policy, content that breaks the WhatsApp Business Policy, threatening messages and requests for sensitive identifiers. Never ask for a password or a full card number in a template.

Length and format

The template body has a character limit, and the number of emojis can be limited too. Tight text gets approved more easily and gets read to the end.

Duplicates

A template with the same wording as one you already have gets rejected. If you want a new version, actually change the wording.

Before and after: fixed templates you can copy

These examples are hypothetical, but the problems are the ones I see most.

1. The variable at the end

Before (ends with a variable):

Hi {{1}}! Your order has shipped. Tracking code: {{2}}

After:

Hi {{1}}! Your order has shipped and your tracking code is {{2}}. If you have any questions about the delivery, just reply to this message.

2. The reminder with a promo tacked on

Before (this will become marketing):

Hi {{1}}. Your appointment is confirmed for {{2}} at {{3}}. While you're here, book a cleaning with 15% off!

After, as utility:

Hi {{1}}. Your appointment at Bright Smile Dental is confirmed for {{2}} at {{3}}. If you need to reschedule, reply to this message by the day before.

The cleaning offer becomes a separate marketing template, sent only to people who agreed to receive offers.

3. Too many blanks

Before:

{{1}}, {{2}} {{3}} {{4}}.

After:

Hi {{1}}. The quote for your sofa is ready: {{2}}, with delivery in {{3}} business days. Want me to hold the fabric you picked?

4. The payment reminder that sounds like a threat

Before:

FINAL NOTICE: pay invoice {{1}} today or your account goes to collections and we will take legal action.

After:

Hi {{1}}. Invoice {{2}}, due on {{3}}, still shows as open on our side. If you've already paid, please ignore this. If you need a new copy, just reply.

Notice that every "after" makes sense on its own. Someone reading the template without the data filled in understands what will be sent. That's what the reviewer needs to see, which is why you should fill in each variable's sample with realistic data when you submit.

My utility template became marketing. Now what?

This can happen at two moments.

At approval. You pick utility, Meta decides it's marketing and approves it as marketing. This is now the default behavior, without you having to allow the change.

After approval. Meta reviews approved templates and can change the category of those that don't follow the guidelines. Per the documentation, there's normally one day's notice. In cases of abuse, the change can happen without notice. You have 60 days to appeal in Business Support Home.

Why does it matter? Money. In Brazil, on the rate card in effect since October 2026, a marketing message costs about nine times a utility message. A reminder sent to 2,000 patients a month changes price a lot. I worked through the full math in the guide on how much the WhatsApp Business API costs.

The fix is almost always the same: remove the promotional part, keep only the information the customer needs, and submit a new template as utility.

How to appeal a rejected template

You have two paths:

  1. Edit and resubmit. Fastest when the reason is obvious, like a variable at the end. Fix it and submit again, ideally under a new name (for example, appointment_reminder_v2).
  2. Appeal. Makes sense when you believe the template is right. Meta requires a filled-in sample with the appeal and says it decides within 24 hours.

If a template was approved and later disabled, Meta lets you edit it and resubmit it for approval.

Advice from someone who has wasted time on appeals: if you need to explain to the reviewer why the text isn't promotional, it probably is. Rewriting is usually faster than arguing.

Build your template library before you need it

A rejected template is rarely a writing problem. It's a calendar problem: you discover it at the exact moment it needed to work.

This week, block half an hour and write the five templates your business uses most: confirmation, reminder, invoice copy, quote follow-up, and one marketing message for past customers. Submit them all at once. When the 24-hour window closes on a Saturday afternoon, you'll thank yourself.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Meta take to approve a WhatsApp template?

According to Meta's documentation, a decision can take up to 24 hours. Many templates are reviewed much faster, but plan for a day of slack.

Can I reuse the name of a rejected template?

The safest move is to create the corrected template with a new name, for example by adding _v2. That avoids clashing with the old one and keeps your list tidy.

Why was my utility template approved as marketing?

Because Meta decided it has promotional intent or isn't tied to something the customer did. A coupon, an invitation to buy or vague text is enough. Remove the promotional part and submit it again as utility.

What if I disagree with the new category?

You can request a review in Business Support Home. For category changes made after approval, Meta gives you 60 days to appeal.
