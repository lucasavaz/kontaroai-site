The customer who messages at 11 pm: after-hours service without hiring more people

A big share of your messages arrive when you're closed. The real problem isn't one night's delay: it's the Saturday chat you can no longer reply to normally by Monday.

LVBy Lucas Vaz·November 4, 2026·8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Count how many conversations start while you're closed before deciding anything. It's usually more than it feels like.
  • A good away message says when someone is back, what can be solved now and what the customer can send ahead.
  • At night, AI can handle prices, hours, booking requests and routine questions. Complaints, discounts and payments wait for the team.
  • A Saturday afternoon message falls outside the 24-hour window before Monday morning. Reply on the weekend or you'll need an approved template.
  • The next morning starts with the conversations the AI handed to the team, not in order of arrival.

Tuesday, 11:07 pm. A woman is on her couch. Earlier she saw an ad for your skincare clinic, and only now has time to ask: “how much is a facial, and do you have Saturday openings?” The clinic closed at 7 pm. The message sits there, two gray ticks, until the receptionist arrives at 9 am.

By 9 am, she's already booked somewhere else. Not because the other place is better. Because they answered first.

After-hours customer service is where many small businesses lose customers without noticing, because nobody sees the conversation that never happened. The good news: you can fix most of it without putting anyone on call.

Who messages you when you're closed?

Think about when your customers actually have time to pick up their phone and type calmly. It's rarely 10 am on a Wednesday.

  • The pizzeria at 11 pm: the order comes in as the kitchen is closing, and the question is whether there's still time.
  • The furniture store on Sunday afternoon: a couple is out walking, saw a sofa on Instagram and wants to know the delivery time.
  • The real estate agency on Saturday night: the family browsed listings all week and only now sat down to message three agents.
  • The dental office at 6:40 am: a patient woke up in pain and wants to know if there's a slot today.
  • The language school at lunchtime: a parent asks about enrollment during the only break they get.

These are high-intent customers. They stopped what they were doing to write. Leaving them unanswered until the next day treats their moment of highest interest as if it were the lowest.

How many messages arrive after hours? Do the math

Before changing anything, measure. Take the new conversations from the last two weeks and mark how many started while you were closed. A spreadsheet with two columns (date and time of the first message) is enough.

To get a sense of scale, here's a hypothetical example. A clinic opens Monday to Friday, 9 am to 7 pm, and Saturday 9 am to 1 pm. That's 54 open hours in a 168-hour week. The clinic is closed 68% of the time.

Of course messages aren't spread evenly: almost nobody writes at 3 am. But suppose that, after counting, the clinic finds this:

PeriodNew chats per weekWait until the team
Business hours150Minutes
Weeknights559 to 14 hours
Saturday afternoon and Sunday35Up to 44 hours
Total24090 after hours (37%)

If half of those 90 conversations are someone asking about price or availability, that's 45 people a week waiting all night for an answer the front desk would give in 30 seconds. Swap in your own numbers. Yours may be higher or lower, but it's rarely zero.

What your away message should say (and what it shouldn't)

If you don't have AI yet, an automatic away message is the bare minimum. And most are badly written. Compare.

The usual version:

Hello! We are currently outside business hours. We will get back to you soon. Thank you for reaching out!

It doesn't say when “soon” is, doesn't help the customer get anything moving, and sounds like every other business. Now a useful version:

Hi! This is Glow Skin Clinic. The front desk is back tomorrow at 9 am and answers overnight messages in order of arrival.

To speed things up, tell me: which treatment are you interested in, and which day of the week works best? That way we can reply with an available time.

For a store with a website:

Good evening! The team is back tomorrow at 9 am. In the meantime, prices and delivery times are on our website for each product. If your question is about an order you already placed, send me the order number and we'll start with you.

And for businesses where something may really be urgent:

Hi! We're closed right now and back at 8 am. If this is an emergency, please go to the nearest emergency room. If it can wait, leave your message and we'll reply first thing.

The rule of three: when you're back (day and time), what the customer can send ahead, where to go for what can't wait. What to leave out: promises your team doesn't keep, prices that change every week, and long blocks listing your hours for each day.

What AI can handle on its own at night

An away message informs. An AI actually serves. An AI trained on your price list, hours, service areas and policies answers the 11:07 pm question immediately, and the facial customer leaves the chat knowing the price with a booking request logged.

But not everything should be settled in the middle of the night. A split that works for most businesses:

The rule is simple: if a mistake would cost money, health or trust, the AI doesn't decide. It logs the request, tells the customer when the team will reply and leaves a summary. There's more on this split in the guide on when AI answers and when it hands off to a person.

If the customer sends a voice note, it transcribes it and can also reply by voice, which helps a lot at night, when many people would rather talk than type.

How the AI should sound when it hands off

The customer needs to know they were heard and when they'll get an answer. For example:

Got it, and I'm sorry about the delivery problem. I've noted everything for the team, who start at 9 am. You'll be one of the first people they get back to tomorrow.

The weekend trap: the 24-hour window

Here's the detail that catches a lot of people. On the official WhatsApp Business API, when a customer messages you, a 24-hour window opens for you to reply freely. After that, you can only start the conversation with a Meta-approved message template. The rule is on Meta's official pricing page, which explains the customer service window and the rates.

Run the numbers for Saturday. The customer writes at 2 pm; you closed at 1 pm. The window expires Sunday at 2 pm. On Monday at 9 am, you can't simply reply “hi, about the sofa…”. You'll need an approved template, which costs more and sounds more formal. If the point of the message is to sell, it will probably fall into the marketing category. The full explanation is in the guide to the WhatsApp 24-hour window.

When the AI replies on Saturday itself, the problem disappears: the question is answered inside the window, and if the customer writes back, the window renews.

And what does replying at night cost?

Nothing extra for being at night. Replies inside the window are service messages. Since October 1, 2026, each number gets 1,000 free service messages per month. From the 1,001st, Meta charges its official rate, which depends on the recipient's country. For Brazil, for example, our calculator uses US$0.0068 per delivered message (rate card of Oct 1, 2026), so a thousand replies beyond the free tier cost about US$6.80. Rates can change every quarter, so check the Meta cost calculator before making plans.

The next-morning routine

A well-handled night only pays off if the morning starts organized. A sequence that works:

  1. First 15 minutes: open the conversations the AI handed to the team. Read the summary, not the whole thread, and answer the sensitive ones first (complaints, payments).
  2. Next: confirm the bookings and orders the AI pre-registered. The customer asked for Saturday morning? Send the exact time.
  3. Then: check conversations whose 24-hour window closes in the next few hours. Someone who wrote yesterday at 10 am needs a reply before 10 am today.
  4. Last: note the questions the AI couldn't answer and add the answers to its knowledge. By tonight, it will know.

At a clinic, this fits in the front desk's first 30 minutes. Mondays take longer because of the weekend, so it's worth starting a bit earlier or splitting the queue between two people. For the rest of the day, the guide on answering customers faster has an hour-by-hour routine.

Start with one night

Don't try to build perfect 24/7 support in one go. Tonight, before you close, replace your away message with one that says when the team is back and what the customer can send ahead. Tomorrow morning, count how many conversations came in overnight.

If that number bothers you, the next step is teaching the AI to answer the five questions that show up most in those messages. It's not much work, and the 11:07 pm customer gets her answer while she's still on the couch.

Frequently asked questions

How can I offer 24/7 support on WhatsApp without someone awake?

Use an AI trained on your business information to answer routine questions and log everything else. It tells customers when the team is back and hands off conversations that need a person, with a summary, for the next morning.

What should a WhatsApp away message say?

When someone will be back (day and time), what the customer can send ahead to be served faster and, if relevant, where to go for something urgent. Avoid “we'll get back to you soon”, which tells them nothing.

Does replying to customers at night on WhatsApp cost more?

Not because it's night. Replies within 24 hours of the customer's last message are service messages: each number gets 1,000 free per month, and after that Meta charges its official rate, the same as at any other time.

Can I reply on Monday to a message that arrived on Saturday?

If more than 24 hours have passed since the customer's last message, only with a Meta-approved message template. That's why it pays to reply over the weekend, even if it's the AI doing it.
